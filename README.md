# Inkling

A novel-writing app inspired by Scrivener, with a virtual pet that cheers you on as your word count grows.

## Features

- **Binder:** chapters and scenes with reordering, status (Draft / Revised / Final) and word counts.
- **Editor:** distraction-free page with focus mode and adjustable text size; bold, italics and underline (buttons or Ctrl+B/I/U), and curly quotes, em dashes and ellipses as you type.
- **Writing tools:** Tab indents (with several lines selected, all of them) and Shift+Tab takes an indent off; a new line keeps the indent of the one before. Undo and redo for each scene (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y or ⌘+Shift+Z, or the buttons), the selected words counted next to the scene's total, typewriter scrolling that keeps the line you're writing in the middle of the screen, *Read aloud* (hear the selection or from the caret on, each paragraph highlighted as it's read: a good way to catch mistakes), Ctrl+F for find and replace, and a list of keyboard shortcuts in the menu.
- **Sticky notes:** stick notes to yourself beside the page of a scene. Drag them up and down or across to the other margin, recolour, edit and delete them. On a narrow screen they fold into coloured tabs on the page edge.
- **Story insights:** give each scene a point-of-view character, a place, a tension level (1–5) and the plot threads it moves on, right in the Inkling panel. Story insights then shows a pacing graph across the book (tension, scene length and the mix of dialogue, action and reflection, estimated from your words where you haven't set it), quiet stretches and where the most intense scene falls; who holds the point of view and each character's screen time chapter by chapter; a plot-thread grid that warns when a subplot goes missing for chapters or is never resolved; and a foreshadowing log: plant a setup (select words in a scene to use them), choose where it pays off, and see which are left hanging or pay off before they're set up.
- **Character arcs:** each character page has an arc tracker: whether they grow, fall or hold firm, their goal, wound, the lie they believe and the truth they need, and a chart where you click to mark how far along their change they are in each chapter (with a note on what changes). Story insights › Arcs shows every arc on one chart, and which point-of-view characters have none yet.
- **World-building:** two more kinds of story page, off until you want them: **Rules & magic** (how a magic system, technology or law works, what it costs, its limits and who can use it) and a **Glossary** of invented words, which are linked in your scenes like names and show their pronunciation and meaning when you point at them. **Calendars & money** (in the menu) lets you invent calendars (months, days, weekdays, an era) with a day-number-to-date converter, use one as a timeline's time scale, and invent currencies with a converter between coins.
- **World maps:** add a World map from Notes & ideas. Upload a map you drew, scanned or made in a map maker (or start on blank parchment), draw on it with a pen, and drop pins for your places, either linked to story pages or as free labels. Each place pin lists the scenes set there; pan and zoom with the mouse or trackpad; story pages show which maps they're on.
- **Series bible:** name the series and the book's number, then bring characters, places, rules, glossary words, timelines, world maps, calendars and money in from another book (another project on this computer, or any backup file), with their pictures. Pages that already exist are updated rather than duplicated, pages keep track of the book they came from, and you can check that book again later for changes. Export everything as one story bible document (Word or Markdown).
- **Continuity check:** give a story page key facts (eyes: green, hair: auburn, age: 34) and Inkling flags scenes that disagree. It also spots names that look misspelled, characters who drop out of the story, and pages never mentioned; each page shows where its name is first and last seen, and the binder shows how often each name appears.
- **Drag and drop:** rearrange scenes and chapters in the binder, cards on the corkboard, outline lines (with everything under them), brainstorm ideas, and the order of timelines, outlines and notes.
- **Corkboard:** index cards with editable synopses, per chapter or for the whole book. Each chapter has its own notes above its cards.
- **Your own section names:** rename Characters, Places, Things and Timelines in the binder (say *Cast*, *Realms*, *Relics*).
- **Outlines:** a nestable outline with numbering, checkboxes, notes and scene links per line, keyboard shortcuts, and templates (three-act, Save the Cat, Hero's Journey, seven-point, romance beats). It can also be filled from your chapters and scenes.
- **Notes and brainstorming:** free-form notes pages and brainstorm boards of colour-coded idea cards.
- **Relationship maps (optional):** bubbles for characters, places, things or anything else, joined by lines you can label. Relationship types (family, romance, rivals, your own) each have a colour and line style and can be hidden. Drag to arrange, drag a bubble's + onto another to connect.
- **Family trees (optional):** add parents, partners, children and siblings and the tree lays itself out. Partnership types have their own symbols and lines; children can be by birth, adopted, fostered, step or rumoured.
- **Your binder, your way:** turn Outlines, Notes, Timelines, Relationship maps, Family trees, Places and Things on or off with *Customize binder*.
- **Timelines:** as many as you like, with lanes for characters or plotlines. Drag cards along the scale, stretch them to show how long something lasts, and stack eras or act markers behind them. The time scale is yours to define: days, years, chapters, or a made-up calendar like `{name}, year {cycle}` with your own month names. Click any column in the header row to rename it, for example *The Siege* instead of *Day 4*. Lanes can be renamed by clicking their name, and can say what they track. Cards can have colours, styles, icons, notes with auto-linked names, and a link to a scene.
- **Story bible:** character, place and thing pages with fill-in templates and a picture at the top (character art, a map, a mood board) with its own caption, shape and size; drag the picture inside its frame and zoom to choose exactly what shows.
- **Auto-linking:** any name or alias that has a page is highlighted in your scenes. Click it to open that page; each page lists the scenes where it appears. Select text and use *Link selection* to create a page.
- **Focus timer:** a pomodoro timer next to Inky with your own focus and break lengths, a per-session word goal and a chime. After the last focus session before a long break, a **writing report** shows your words per minute for each session and on average, your best session, whether you sped up or slowed down, how that compares with your usual pace, and how many hours of focus the rest of your novel would take at that pace. Reports for earlier runs of sessions open from *Writing sessions*. Every session is saved with its new words, and **Writing sessions** shows your history for the project, with totals, per-day groups, and deleting by session, day or all.
- **Inky, the pet:** reacts to your daily goal (25/50/75/100%, bonus words, streaks) and grows from Blot to Inkling, Scribe, Bard and Laureate as you write toward your novel length goal (5%, 25%, 50% and 100% of it). Inky notices what you do (a new character or place, a finished chapter, a scene passing 1,000 words, writing at dawn or after midnight, coming back after a break) and lives in a little room behind it: the window follows the time of day, a plant grows with your streak, and stars on the wall count your goal days.
- **Inky reads over your shoulder:** while you write, Inky reads the lines around the cursor and reacts. The story's weather, time of day and setting show in the window of Inky's room: rain, snow, fog, a storm with lightning, night or dawn, the sea, a forest, a city, mountains, a castle. The scene's mood and what just happened show on Inky's face and in its paws: an umbrella in the rain, a tissue when someone cries, popcorn for a fight, hearts for a kiss, a magnifying glass for a secret. Now and then Inky says something, using the names in the passage ("Run, Mara, run!"), and when you open a scene it reminds you where you were ("Where were we? Mara, at Harrow Point, at night, in the rain."). Inky also notices a word repeated in a few lines and a fact that disagrees with a character's page. Inky understands negation ("not afraid" isn't fear) and weighs the latest sentences most. In focus mode or on a phone, a small Inky peeks up from the corner of the page. It can be switched off in Inky's room. Everything happens on your own device.
- **Today's to-do list:** under the daily goal. Add tasks, tick them off, drag to reorder; tap an idea to add it. Some tick themselves: *Write 500 words* when you reach your goal, *Do a focus sprint* when a session ends, *Finish "a scene"* when it is marked Final. Unfinished tasks carry over to the next day. It is yours alone on a shared project.
- **Inky's room:** 22 achievements earn hats, glasses, scarves, a bow tie, a medal, a mug of ink, a clipboard (tick off 25 to-do tasks), and furniture for the room (a lamp, a rug, a map, an armchair, a bookshelf that fills with finished chapters). Each project has its own Inky. Dress Inky up, pick a colour, and choose how chatty Inky is (chatty, only big moments, or quiet), with or without confetti.
- **Sprint together:** on a shared project, you see when a co-writer starts a focus sprint, and can join it so both timers end together; their Inky appears beside yours while you sprint.
- **Book details (Publishing):** everything a finished book needs around the story, on one screen (menu › Book details): subtitle, imprint, edition, ISBNs, description and keywords; front matter (half title, title page, a copyright page written for you from those details or in your own words, dedication, epigraph, contents) and back matter (acknowledgments, about the author, also by, links to your newsletter or store pages), each switched on or off and dragged into order; and chapter headings (Chapter One, Chapter 1, One, 1 or titles only). Prologues and epilogues can be left unnumbered, and part dividers (“Part One”) group chapters (right-click a chapter in the binder). Exports include the front and back matter when ticked (always for EPUB), and EPUBs carry the details, a linked contents page and landmarks for Kindle and other stores.
- **Export:** Word (.docx) and PDF in standard manuscript format (12 pt Times or Courier, double spaced, 1" margins, a *Surname / TITLE / page* header, a title page with your contact details and a rounded word count), an EPUB ebook to read your draft on a Kindle or phone, Markdown and plain text. Export the whole book or one chapter. `*Asterisks*` or `_underscores_` make italics.
- **Scene snapshots:** save a version of a scene before a rewrite, compare any snapshot with the current text (added and removed words highlighted) and restore it. Inkling also keeps one automatically each day you change a scene, and before find and replace or a restore changes it.
- **Find and replace:** across the whole book or one chapter, with match case and whole words. Click a result to jump to it in the scene. Replace one at a time or all at once (each changed scene is snapshotted first).
- **Writing history:** a chart of new words per day (30 or 90 days) or per week (12 months), with totals, best day, streak and days at goal, and a table view.
- **Sharing with co-writers (desktop app):** keep a project in a Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive or iCloud Drive folder and several people can work on it from their own computers. Inkling merges everyone's changes piece by piece, shows who is in which scene, keeps each writer's pet and goals separate, and saves the other version of any scene two people changed at once as a snapshot. See [SHARING.md](SHARING.md).
- **Deadline planner:** set a goal and a date ("finish my draft at 80,000 words by March 1", or "write 50,000 new words in a month" with the **One Book a Month** button) and the days of the week you write. Inkling works out how many words to write each day and makes that your daily goal. Today's goal stays put while you write; the next day it adjusts, spreading missed words over the days left or easing off when you got ahead. It shows whether you're on track, ahead or behind and by how much, your average per writing day and the date you'd finish at that pace, a chart of your progress against the plan, and a calendar where you can click a coming day to take it off. Inky mentions the plan when you open the project and celebrates when you reach it.
- **Trash:** deleted scenes, chapters, story pages, notes, outlines, timelines, maps and trees wait in the Trash for 30 days (with their snapshots, pictures and map links), with an *Undo* right after deleting and *Restore* or *Delete forever* in the Trash (bottom of the binder, or the menu). On a shared project everyone sees the same Trash.
- **Search everything:** one search (Ctrl+K, or the box at the top of the binder) across scene text, titles, synopses, notes and sticky notes, chapter notes, story pages, notes pages and brainstorm ideas, outlines, timeline cards, and map and tree notes. Results show where each match is, with the words around it; filter by kind, match case or whole words, and click to jump straight to it.
- **Side by side:** open a story page, a notes page, an outline, a timeline or another scene next to the page you're writing (the split button above a scene, Alt+click in the binder, or Ctrl+\\). Story page fields and notes can be edited right there; ⇄ swaps the two sides.
- **Getting around:** each scene reopens where you left it (cursor and scroll position), and a project reopens on the last thing you had open. Ctrl+P (⌘P) opens a quick switcher: type a few letters of any scene, chapter, page, note, outline, timeline, map or tree, or a screen like the Trash, and press Enter (Alt+Enter opens it side by side). Arrows under each scene, or Alt+Page Down / Page Up, go to the next or previous scene. Back and forward work like a browser: Alt+← / Alt+→ (⌘[ / ⌘] on a Mac), your mouse's back and forward buttons, and buttons at the top of the page. The desktop app has a Go menu with all of these.
- **Right-click in the binder:** open, open side by side, rename, duplicate, move to another chapter, change status, set a word target, change a page's type, or move to the Trash. The ⌃ button above the chapters collapses or expands them all.
- **Word targets:** give a scene or a chapter a target (right-click, or *Word target* beside a scene). A thin bar under it in the binder fills as you write, the word count above the page shows "540 of 1,000 words", and Inky cheers when you reach it.
- **Your own story page fields:** *Edit fields* on any character, place or thing page adds, renames, reorders or removes fields for every page of that type in the project (say *Magic ability* or *Faction*).
- **Pictures by drag and drop:** drop an image file onto a story page, or paste one, to set its picture.
- **Window › Project settings:** choose what you see while you work: the binder and the Inkling panel, the word counter and saving status, each button above the page, the tip under the page, Inky's speech bubble and the peeking Inky, and which sections the Inkling panel shows in what order (drag them). Start from a preset (*Everything*, *Calm*, *Just the page*). Each project remembers its own setup and you can make one your default for new projects. The same page sets the title, author and goals. Ctrl+Shift+1, 2 and 3 show or hide the binder, the Inkling panel and focus mode; the desktop app has a Window menu with the same.
- **Settings:** light or dark theme, writing font, text size, line spacing, page width and spellcheck, kept per device.
- **Wider side panels:** drag the edge of the binder or of the Inkling panel to make it wider (or use the arrow keys on it); double-click the edge to put it back. Inkling always leaves room for the page.
- **A short tour** on first run, replayable from the menu.
- **Backup:** the whole project as JSON, pictures included (restore from the menu).

## Desktop app

Inkling also comes as a downloadable app for Windows, macOS and Linux that needs no Claude account or internet, and keeps several projects on the computer, each in its own folder with automatic backups. Put a project in Dropbox, iCloud Drive, Google Drive or OneDrive to back it up online, write on more than one computer, or share it with co-writers ([SHARING.md](SHARING.md)). It tells people when a new version is out and, on Windows and Linux, installs it with one click. See [desktop/README.md](desktop/README.md) for installing and building it, and [RELEASING.md](RELEASING.md) for publishing an update.

## Privacy

Inkling has no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking. The desktop app keeps everything in your projects folder and connects to the internet only to check GitHub for a new version (which you can switch off in Settings). In a browser your project stays in that browser's storage; in the Claude artifact it saves to your Claude account, private to you.

## Run it

The finished app is one static file, `index.html`, with no dependencies. It is built from the source in `src/` (the page, its styles in `src/css/` and its code in `src/js/`, split by feature):

```sh
npm run format                 # tidy the code with Prettier and rebuild index.html
node build.js                  # or just rebuild index.html after changing anything in src/
```

To try it:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000   # then open http://localhost:8000
```

Or open `index.html` directly in a browser. Edit the files in `src/`, never `index.html` itself: the build overwrites it, and the tests check that it matches `src/`. Projects save to `localStorage` in that browser, so use *Back up project* in the menu regularly.

## Tests

```sh
npm install
npx playwright install chromium
npm test                     # builds index.html, then runs the web and desktop tests
npm run test:web             # just the browser ones
```

The desktop tests need `npm ci` in `desktop/` first, and a screen (on Linux servers, run them under `xvfb-run -a`). GitHub Actions runs everything on every push and before each release.
